The Problem

We had the depth. We didn't have the clarity.

Visual — review quote strip / word cloud
(Play Store · App Store · Twitter · Reddit)
Business problem

Rating slipped to 3.4 and became the team's top priority — not one broken feature, but the app failing in dozens of small ways that added up.

Root cause

Clutter, sluggish performance, and buggy video/notifications compounded — real strengths like StatsGuru and predictors existed but were buried and invisible.

Band-aid

Individual fixes kept landing in isolation — a nav tweak here, a bug patch there — without a shared read on what was actually driving the drop.

Strategy

Diagnose from real user signal first, then let design — not instinct — decide what stays, what goes, and what gets rebuilt.

Overwhelming / cluttered Buggy video playback Inflexible notifications Cluttered bottom nav Slow, laggy performance Live scores lag competitors High data consumption Losing younger audience

The Reframe

The rating wasn't the design problem. It was the signal. My role was to turn that complexity into a product that felt simpler — without making it less powerful.

The Decisions

What we changed — and why

Each of these is a real trade-off, made on purpose — told as what we gave up and why, not as a features list.

01

Home page decluttering

Same information, less noise
  • Featured articles cut from 7 to 5
  • Removed a redundant "In-Depth Stories" section — the same ~40 stories were repeating across three modules for one match
  • Removed the filler "Top Players / Top Tournaments" section
  • Rule: one featured-story module visible at a time
  • Added a lightweight match-prediction game for engagement
Trade-offWe didn't rebuild the home page — a full redesign would have cost more time than the clutter actually warranted, so this stayed a decluttering pass, not a rethink.

02

Match page structure

Same clutter problem, one level deeper
  • Match pages had grown to 8–15 tabs with no governing structure
  • Full IA breakdown — match-state relevance, content snacking, navigation — lives in the Match Page Experience case study
Trade-offThis got its own case study rather than a summary here — the material was too deep to compress without losing what made it work.

03

Video & short-form content

Fit the format to the content, not the other way around
  • Fixed buggy long-form playback; muted autoplay audio by default
  • Added a playlist for continuous viewing
  • Introduced Quick Reads, short videos, and Stories — for the ~80% of users who weren't reading full articles at all
Trade-offShort-form wasn't a trend chase — highlights and reactions are inherently bite-sized, and forcing them into a long-form frame was the actual mismatch.

04

Notifications & navigation

Reliability first, then flexibility
  • Stabilized notification reliability
  • Moved away from a one-size-fits-all model toward more flexible, less intrusive alerts
  • Simplified bottom navigation to cut down on competing entry points

05

Lightweight engagement

A lower-effort way to feel part of a match
  • Live polls, fan ratings, in-app games, share — sitting alongside deep stats, not replacing them
  • Reduced ad clutter and standardized ad sizes for visual cohesion
Trade-offThe goal was never to dumb the product down — it was to give casual fans a way in that didn't require expertise, without asking core fans to give up any depth.

Alongside the Design Work

What engineering fixed in parallel

Not the headline — but they mattered enough to name honestly, and they're the reason the app size and speed numbers moved too.

Performance

Font & layout architecture

  • Custom downloadable font → native system fonts
  • Nested layout → flat, linear rendering structure
  • Live-score load-speed fix to close the gap with faster competitors
Outcome

What it bought us

  • App size down 30%, tied directly to the font and layout changes
  • A lighter, less cluttered feel as a side effect of a performance fix — not a design decision on its own

The interesting part of this story isn't that the app got faster — it's the judgment calls that decided what the faster app should actually look and behave like.

The Result
3.4 → 4.5

Across 10M+ users — downloads up, app size down 30%, time spent on the app up 50%. Not a one-time fix — we kept watching app behavior for weeks after launch and iterated as new signal came in. Clarity is a design responsibility, not a subtraction exercise.
